The lighthouse had been inventoried incorrectly: three jars of fog, one brass comb, a birdcage containing only the sound of wings, and a stairwell that descended whenever Mara climbed it. She checked each item twice, because the island’s auditors were gulls in waistcoats and took offense at arithmetic. At midnight, the lens flashed not seaward but inward, illuminating a tiny theater behind Mara’s left eye. On its velvet stage, a drowned map unrolled itself and pointed to a room she had never built, where someone was patiently knocking from inside the wall.
