By dawn, the palindrome snow had fallen upward again, leaving the market stalls roofed in clean black sky and the cobbles spelling the same warning from either end. Mara turned her collar against the rising flakes and counted three impossible things on the fishmonger's slab: a trout with brass eyelashes, a price tag written in her own childhood hand, and a tiny blue door hinged beneath the ice where the gills should have been. When she touched the latch, every bell in the square rang once, and someone inside the fish whispered her name backward.
