Aram, who sold gloves stitched for hands not yet born, heard the bells and dropped a pair that crawled away on its fingers. He watched Mara kneel before the trout's tiny blue door while the black sky on the stall roofs reflected a noon that had not happened. From the gills came her backward name again, followed by the scrape of a chair, and then a moth the size of a saucer squeezed through the latch carrying a wet envelope. Its wings were printed with a map of the market, except one alley pulsed red where no alley had stood that morning.
