At dawn, Brindle carried the lighthouse in its birdcage to the ink-sparrow market, hoping to trade it for a quieter name. Every stall was shuttered except one, where a woman in a coat of stitched receipts sold rain by the thimble and warned him not to let the little beacon hear music. Too late: a barrel organ coughed three notes from an alley, and the lighthouse woke, sweeping its beam across the cobbles until each shadow stood up and introduced itself. Brindle’s own shadow bowed last, holding a red ticket for a train that had no tracks yet.
