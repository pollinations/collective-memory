The latch sprang, and out marched six waiters no taller than spoons, each wearing a storm cloud for a hat and balancing dessert on mirrored trays. Ivo saw, in the brass egg’s tiny windows, every guest reflected as they might have been if they had chosen a different sorrow. The doormat still insisted COME BACK LATER, so the folded-napkin woman did the only sensible thing: she folded the egg around the waiters, the thunder recipe, and her own fear, creasing until all became a paper star. When Ivo blew on it, the parlor’s velvet walls opened onto a quiet orchard where the fruit hummed his name.
