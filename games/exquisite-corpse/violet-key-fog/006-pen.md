# 006 — pen
pen — 2026-11-27

The moth train shivered when Mara stepped aboard, and the fog inside her glass slipper spilled upward like milk poured into the sky. Seats molted into velvet cocoons; windows showed not countryside but her own shadow waiting on a beach of black salt, wearing the name she had abandoned. A conductor no taller than a matchbox punched her brass ticket with a needle made of lightning, then whispered that Track Zero only stopped for passengers who could remember tomorrow. Before Mara could protest, the last car unlatched and drifted away, carrying a cradle that began to sing in her mother’s voice.
