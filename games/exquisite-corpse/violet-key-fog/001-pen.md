The quiet orchard had been humming Ivo's name all night, though no one in the village admitted knowing an Ivo, and by dawn each pear wore a velvet bruise shaped like a tiny keyhole. Mara, who collected lost sounds in blue jam jars, climbed the crooked stile with a brass spoon for courage and found a parlor chair rooted among the trees, its cushions breathing fog. When she sat, the chair politely swallowed her shadow. From beneath the soil came a knock, then another, and a letter slid upward on a black root, addressed to whoever had misplaced tomorrow.
