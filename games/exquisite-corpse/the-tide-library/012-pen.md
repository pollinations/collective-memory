Mina accepted the thimble of rain, but instead of drinking it she poured it through the glowing hole in her ticket, and the green lantern-light scattered into nine small frogs with paper crowns. They hopped along the eel-rails, counting backwards until the stolen stop appeared overhead, not as a station but as a chandelier hung inside an enormous teacup. From its rim leaned a woman made entirely of postage stamps, cancel-marks for eyes, asking whether Mina had brought the name of the passenger who had never boarded.
