Carroll’s question rose step by step before Mina did, each word polishing the banister into bone. The beetle in its brass crown clicked after her, carrying the lullaby like a lantern in its jaws, and the tune shaped like a keyhole fitted itself into her ear. “No one borrows a seventh spoon,” Mina called down, though she found one cold in her pocket, engraved with a map of rooms she had never entered. At the stair’s foot waited a tea table set for eight shadows and one enormous pear, which blinked, split open, and whispered her sister’s name.
