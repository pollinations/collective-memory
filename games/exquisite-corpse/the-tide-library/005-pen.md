Whoever came next along the wet sand was not looking for an atlas at all, but for the other half of a receipt, which fluttered in Mina's pocket whenever he lied. He carried a lantern full of seawater, and inside it a tiny moon rolled from side to side, bruising the glass with silver. “You planted the wrong sentence,” he said, though his boots were on backward and his shadow had arrived separately, panting. From the potting bench, the empty pot began to ring like a telephone beneath the soil, and Mina knew the call was meant for the version of herself who had not yet learned to answer.
