Mara almost answered with the name of her missing brother, but the fox in the porter’s cap sniffed and shook its head, as if absences had to be declared in a stricter grammar. Behind him, the flowers clicked their room keys together, a nervous chandelier of tiny brass teeth. “I came for whatever keeps ringing,” she said, and the garden path folded into a carpeted hallway ankle-deep in seawater. From beneath the surface rose a porcelain hand, offering her a damp envelope sealed with blue wax. On it, in the doorbell’s smallest voice, someone had written OK, OK, OK.
